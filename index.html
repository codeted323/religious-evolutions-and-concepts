How this was built, and its limits

Each tradition is coded for 100 traits, grouped into 7 categories and 30 subgroups. The coding describes its mainstream or normative form; real communities vary widely, and one entry cannot capture every voice inside a faith. Each tradition descends from one main ancestor (solid lines) and can draw on others (dashed blend links). Like a species, a tradition inherits its parent's traits unless it gains or drops them. 'Gained here' marks where a trait enters a lineage.

  • Dates mark when a tradition became recognizably distinct, not every claimed origin. Many are approximate. Confidence (high, medium, low) reflects how secure the dating and ancestry are. Prehistoric and reconstructed nodes (such as Proto-Indo-European religion) are scholarly inferences.
  • Academic, not devotional, history. Origins follow mainstream scholarship. Many traditions understand their own beginnings differently. For example, many Hindus see their dharma as eternal, and many Jews, Christians and Muslims date their faith to earlier revelation.
  • Similarity is the Jaccard index: shared traits divided by all traits either has. The belief-space map arranges traditions by multidimensional scaling of these scores.
  • Simplification. The tree model is a tool, not a verdict. Religions blend far more than species, so read the blend links as part of the picture.
